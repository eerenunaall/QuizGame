# Live quiz nights: how they are run, priced and booked (Turkey vs UK/US)

> Research date: 2026-11-14. Method note: WebFetch could not reach the primary pages in this cloud session (yabangee.com, tayfaistanbul.com and biletino.com returned "host not allowed"). Every finding below therefore comes from **search-engine result snippets and summaries of the listed pages**, not from reading the full pages. The figures are concrete and consistent across several listings, but each should be re-checked against the live page before anyone relies on it. TL prices are given in nominal lira with their date. The Beyoğlu Trivia Night series shows how fast they move: 50 TL in 2022, 200 TL in August 2025 and 250 TL in December 2025 (sources below).

## Q1. Turkish quiz nights (Istanbul, Ankara, Izmir, Çanakkale): price, rounds and questions, prizes, registration

### Takeaway
Turkey has a busy, ticketed quiz-night scene that is mostly **themed** (a TV series, film or pop-culture franchise) and runs in cafés and pubs. Entry is per person, usually **200–400 TL in 2025–2026**, and tickets are sold on Biletino, Bubilet, biletimGO, Biletix or Biletinial, or paid at the door. Prizes are small and in kind (free coffees, a "surprise gift"), not cash. A typical night is about **40–50 questions in two halves with a 15-minute break**, read aloud by a moderator, with teams of up to about 6 and solo players welcome.

### Inferences
- The dominant Turkish model is a **ticketed, themed, per-person event**: a quiz organiser (The Quiz Society, Sanatco.Ankara, Yabangee) rents a café or pub slot, sells tickets on a ticketing platform, and the venue makes its money on food and drink. This is unlike the UK, where the quiz is a free or near-free loss leader for the bar.
- 2026 per-person prices cluster at **200–400 TL**, with a student tier around 200 TL. At the TL rates implied by the US$7–14 aggregator price, that is roughly the price of one or two café drinks.
- Prizes are symbolic (free coffees, a surprise gift, bragging rights). Cash prizes are explicitly avoided by at least one major series.
- Booking is mostly **paid presale on Biletino, Bubilet, biletimGO or Biletix**, with door payment (cash) also common and unnumbered seating. Solo walk-ins are absorbed by merging them into teams.
- The common shape is **40–50 questions in two halves with about 15 minutes of interval**, running about 2–2.5 hours. That is fewer questions than a typical UK night of 60–80.

## Q2. UK and US norms (format, pacing, marking, prizes, fees, team limits, phones)

### Takeaway
The UK pub quiz has **6–8 rounds of 8–10 questions** (about 60–80 questions), takes **90–120 minutes of actual quizzing** with a mid-way break, and costs **£1–2 per person**, often with a rolling cash jackpot. Questions are read twice, and ties are settled by a "closest number" question. US bar trivia, as run by Geeks Who Drink or Trivia Mafia, is typically **free to enter**, uses about 8 rounds, caps teams at 6, and gives **bar gift cards** to the top 2–3 teams.

### Cited Findings
- UK structure: about **5–6 rounds of 8–10 questions**, with rounds of **12–15 minutes** and breaks for the bar. Alternatively **6–8 rounds of 10 questions over about 2 hours, with a mid-way break**. Typical rounds are themed, music, picture and local knowledge. — [Orange Jelly, Quiz Night 101](https://www.orangejelly.co.uk/licensees-guide/quiz-night-101); [Pub quiz, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pub_quiz)
- UK timeline example: the quiz runs **19:30–21:20**, with the **tie-break and winners at 21:10**. Printed answer sheets that include the tie-break line make marking faster. — [Orange Jelly, Quiz Night 101](https://www.orangejelly.co.uk/licensees-guide/quiz-night-101)
- Reading rule: **read every question twice, slowly, and never take a third request from one table**. Aim for **90–120 minutes of actual quizzing**. Music clips run **10–20 seconds**. — [Orange Jelly, Quiz Night Ideas](https://www.orangejelly.co.uk/guides/quiz-night-ideas)
- Tie-break: **"a question, never a draw"**. No coin tosses or names from a hat when there is a prize; use a "closest to" number question. One UK host collects every team's **tie-break answer at half-time** so it is ready if needed. — [Orange Jelly, Quiz Night Ideas](https://www.orangejelly.co.uk/guides/quiz-night-ideas); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pub_quiz)
- Fees: about **£1 per person (or per sheet)**, and "most successful pub quizzes charge **£1–£2 per person** or include entry with a drink purchase". — [Orange Jelly](https://www.orangejelly.co.uk/licensees-guide/quiz-night-101); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pub_quiz)
- Jackpot: often a separate end-of-quiz round. Money is added each week and **rolls over** if no team answers correctly. The **maximum may be limited by local gaming regulations**. — [Wikipedia, Pub quiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pub_quiz)
- Marking: either the quizmaster marks, or **teams swap sheets with a neighbouring team** at the end of each round. Swapping is described as "divisive". — [Wikipedia, Pub quiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pub_quiz); [The Frosterley Table, British Pub Quiz Guide](https://blackbullfrosterley.com/british-pub-quiz-guide/)
- UK team sizes: typically **4–6**, and large teams are sometimes capped. Some pubs cap teams at **6–10**. — [Orange Jelly](https://www.orangejelly.co.uk/licensees-guide/quiz-night-101); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pub_quiz)
- **Geeks Who Drink (US)**: the official FAQ says "Our team maximum is 6". Larger teams can play but **are not eligible for prizes**. The format is **8 rounds of 8 questions** (some venues 7). Answers are written and **handed in at the end of each round**. Entry is generally free ("with rare exceptions, no"). Prizes are **bar gift certificates** for the top teams, plus bonus-question prizes such as free pints. — [Geeks Who Drink FAQ](https://www.geekswhodrink.com/faq/); [Cornell Events listing](https://events.cornell.edu/event/geeks_who_drink_pub_quiz); [ABC13 Houston](https://abc13.com/geeks-who-drink-pub-trivia-quiz-game-drinking/2194386/)
- Phones: a 2016 student-paper account says Geeks Who Drink teams play **with no phones or notes**. The current GWD FAQ, however, describes people viewing the question text on their phones for inclusivity. The current rule is unclear. — [Rocky Mountain Collegian 2016](https://collegian.com/blogs/2016/10/nightlife-geeks-who-drink/); [Geeks Who Drink FAQ](https://www.geekswhodrink.com/faq/)
- **Trivia Mafia (US) host handbook**: venues typically provide **three prizes, usually gift cards**, for 1st, 2nd, and either 3rd or a fun category such as **best team name**. The venue sets the value. The host presents the questions, grades the sheets, enforces the rules and hands out the prizes. — [Trivia Mafia Host Handbook](https://wiki.triviamafia.com/books/trivia-mafia-host-handbook/page/run-of-show/revisions/66)
- US entry: promotional bar trivia should **not charge entry**, and many NYC bars run it with no cover charge. Some venues allow teams of "1–6 players; hosts may allow up to 8". — [WebstaurantStore, What is a trivia night](https://www.webstaurantstore.com/blog/4324/what-is-a-trivia-night.html); [CBS New York](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/nycs-best-bars-with-trivia/); [Last Call Trivia](https://lastcalltrivia.com/events/barleycorns-brewhouse-wilder-jul-6-2026/)

### Inferences
- In the UK and US the quiz is a **traffic driver for the bar**: entry is free or nominal and the venue pays the host or quiz company. In Turkey the **organiser charges the player** through a ticket.
- Score reveal is per round in the UK and US: sheets are handed in or swapped after each round and the host announces a running leaderboard. This follows from the per-round hand-in rule, but no source gave the exact cadence of leaderboard announcements.
- At about 10 questions in 12–15 minutes, the UK pace is roughly **60–90 seconds per question including the double reading**. This is my derivation from the cited round length, not a figure any source stated.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative **seconds-per-question** figure, and none was found in the Orange Jelly material either.
- I found no **written rules on partial credit or spelling tolerance** for paper quizzes. The only sourced hints come from apps (see Q3). Common practice ("the host's decision is final", "phonetic spelling accepted") is plausible but was not sourced.
- **Last-place prizes**: no source confirmed them.
- I found no figures on host pay or quiz-company fees to venues.
- I found no typical attendance figures for UK or US nights.

## Q3. Host tools and what makes a night run smoothly

### Takeaway
Paper is still standard: printable answer sheets and score grids are widely available free. A growing class of host apps adds **phone answering with automatic marking that tolerates spelling**, **half points**, undo, and a phone-free mode. Experienced hosts stress reading each question twice, keeping printed sheets with a tie-break line, and holding a firm schedule.

### Cited Findings
- Free printable answer and score sheets (A4 PDF, portrait or landscape, with numbered answer spaces) are offered by Justprintables, Real Quiz Company and Pubquizgenerator.co.uk. Pubquizgenerator provides a quizmaster sheet, team sheets and a score sheet. — [Justprintables answer sheets](https://www.justprintables.co.uk/answer-sheets.html); [Real Quiz Company](https://www.realquizcompany.co.uk/scoresheets.html); [Pub Quiz Generator](https://pubquizgenerator.co.uk/)
- **Quizsheetgenerator.com** is a free browser tool that builds a host scoring grid with configurable team rows, round count and round names. **Pubquiznederland.nl** offers three free score sheets, including a 10-round Google Sheets template. — [Quiz Sheet Generator](https://quizsheetgenerator.com/score-sheet-generator/); [Pubquiz Nederland](https://pubquiznederland.nl/en/pubquiz-score-form/)
- **Pub Quiz Generator** lets teams answer on their own phones with **automatic marking that tolerates small spelling slips**, and it has a paper fallback. — [Pub Quiz Generator](https://pubquizgenerator.co.uk/)
- **The Party Quiz** supports team scoring with **half points, steals and undo**, prints a numbered answer sheet for each team, and has a **"phone-free night" setting**. — [The Party Quiz, answer sheets](https://www.thepartyquiz.com/features/answer-sheets)
- Some UK quiz companies have dropped paper for a live app (A Whittle Quiz). — [A Whittle Quiz](https://www.awhittlequiz.co.uk/)
- Practical advice: read questions twice; use a "closest to" tie-break, never a draw; collect tie-break answers at half-time; use printed sheets with a tie-break line for faster marking; plan 90–120 minutes of quiz in a 19:30–21:20 window. — [Orange Jelly, Quiz Night Ideas](https://www.orangejelly.co.uk/guides/quiz-night-ideas); [Orange Jelly, Quiz Night 101](https://www.orangejelly.co.uk/licensees-guide/quiz-night-101)
- Trivia Mafia's run of show has a first half, an **intermission** and a second half. Hosts should state the team-size limit clearly, or skip it when no large teams are present. — [Trivia Mafia, first half](https://wiki.triviamafia.com/books/trivia-mafia-host-handbook/page/first-half/revisions/675); [Trivia Mafia, intermission](https://wiki.triviamafia.com/books/trivia-mafia-host-handbook/page/intermission/revisions/784/changes)

### Inferences
- For a TV-and-phone product, the features that current host apps have converged on (spelling-tolerant auto-marking, half points, undo or host override, a numbered paper fallback, a phone-free mode, a stored tie-break answer) are the minimum hosts will expect.
- Turkish organisers (moderator reads aloud, two halves) appear to run on paper or simple slides. I found no evidence of dedicated Turkish host software.

### Gaps
- I could not compare **SpeedQuizzing, Crowdpurr, Kahoot or TriviaMaker** because the searches returned nothing specific on them.
- I found no forum-sourced (Reddit, quizmaster forum) host testimonials about dispute handling.

## Q4. Board-game cafés in Turkey: pricing model (2025–2026) and handling a walk-in group that wants to play the quiz

Turkish board-game cafés use a mix of models: a **flat game fee per person** (for example 100 TL), a **day or membership fee** (Goblin, Kadıköy), a **minimum spend per person or per table** (a reported 800 TL per table in Üsküdar), and unlimited time once inside. I found no reliable, current price list, and sources are dated or user-generated. Separately, some Turkish cafés have begun charging **table-occupancy fees** (50 TL per half hour without a new order, reported in 2025).

### Inferences
- Walk-in groups who say "we want to play the quiz": at Turkish ticketed quiz nights, solo players and small groups are merged into teams. The Yabangee series explicitly does this, and The Quiz Society allows individual entry. The Yabangee series also takes payment at the door in cash, so walk-ins are accommodated if seats remain; seating is unnumbered. Outside a scheduled quiz night, a café would most plausibly apply its normal model (game fee, minimum spend or table fee). No source describes an on-demand quiz offered to walk-ins.
- A plausible 2026 board-game-café price band is about **100–250 TL per person** in game fee or minimum spend, extrapolated from the 100 TL figures (2024–2025) and the 800 TL per table of four. This is low-confidence.
